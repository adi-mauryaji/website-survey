Is this for you?

You might be here because…

Anxiety and stress show up differently for everyone. Perhaps some of this sounds familiar.

  • Your mind keeps running, especially at night, and it’s hard to switch off.
  • Work, studies or family feel like too much, and you’re tired of holding it all together.
  • Your body feels on edge, with a tight chest, a racing heart or a knot in your stomach, and you can’t always say why.
  • You’ve started avoiding things, like calls, meetings, people or places, because of how they make you feel.
  • You replay conversations, or keep picturing what might go wrong.
  • You’re coping on the outside, but it’s taking more out of you than anyone sees.

You don’t need a diagnosis, or the right words, to begin. Telling me what’s been hard lately is enough.

How I work

Practical, gentle, and shaped around you

I draw on two well-researched approaches to therapy and adapt them to you. There’s no script, and you won’t be handed a worksheet and left to it.

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)

CBT looks at the links between what you think, how you feel and what you do. We notice the patterns that keep anxiety going, such as “what if” thinking or avoiding things, and try out new ways of responding, one small step at a time.

Acceptance-based therapy

Some feelings can’t simply be argued away. Acceptance-based approaches help you make room for difficult thoughts and feelings without being pushed around by them, so more of your energy can go towards what matters to you.

At a pace that suits you

We’ll agree on what you’d like to work on, check in regularly on whether it’s helping, and change course if it isn’t. Some sessions will be practical; some will simply be space to talk.

Questions

Things people often ask

If your question isn’t here, add it to the booking form and I’ll answer it when I reply.

Who do you work with?

Adults (18 and over) who are dealing with anxiety and stress. If what you’re going through needs a different kind of support, I’ll tell you honestly and suggest where to turn.

Is online therapy as helpful as meeting in person?

For many people, yes. Online therapy can work well for anxiety and stress, and some people find it easier to open up from a familiar space. If it doesn’t feel right for you, we can talk about that too.

What do I need for a session?

A phone or laptop with a steady internet connection, a private space where you won’t be overheard, and headphones if you have them. I’ll send you the link for the video call before we meet.

Is what I share kept confidential?

Yes. What you share stays between us. There are a few safety and legal limits to confidentiality, for example if there is a serious risk to your life or someone else’s, and I’ll explain these clearly in our first session.

Which languages can we talk in?

English, Hindi or Marathi, or a mix of them if that’s how you naturally speak.

Do you prescribe medication?

No. As a psychologist, I don’t prescribe medication. If it seems medication might help, we can talk about seeing a psychiatrist alongside our sessions.

Is there help for students who can’t afford the full fee?

Yes. A sliding scale is available for students. Tick the box on the booking form and we’ll agree on a fee privately.

What if I’m in crisis?

This practice isn’t an emergency service. If you’re in crisis or worried about your safety, please call 112 or go to your nearest hospital emergency department. You can also call Tele-MANAS on 14416, free and open 24 hours.
