What we can work on

Anxiety and stress look different for everyone. People often get in touch when:

  • your mind keeps running through what could go wrong, especially at night
  • work, studies or family pressure no longer switches off when the day ends
  • panic arrives without warning: a racing heart, a tight chest, the urge to get out
  • you’ve started avoiding calls, places or decisions because of how they make you feel
  • you’re tired, irritable or sleeping badly, and can’t quite say why
  • you’re coping, but it takes everything you have

You don’t need a diagnosis or a crisis to start. If something has been weighing on you for a while, that is reason enough to talk.

How I work

I use two approaches with good research behind them for anxiety, and shape them around you.

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)

We look at the thoughts, habits and avoidance that keep anxiety going. Then we try out different ways of responding, in sessions and in your week, at a pace you choose.

Acceptance-based therapy

Fighting every anxious feeling tends to make it louder. You learn to make room for difficult feelings, and to keep doing what matters to you while they’re there.

Sessions are a conversation, not a lecture. You decide what we talk about and how fast we go.

Questions people ask

Is what I tell you confidential?

Yes. What you share stays between us. The only exception is if I believe you, or someone else, is at serious risk of harm. Then I may need to involve others, and wherever possible I’ll talk with you about it first.

What do I need for an online session?

A phone or laptop with a steady internet connection, and somewhere you can talk without being overheard. Earphones help. If privacy at home is hard to find, tell me and we’ll work out what’s possible.

Do you prescribe medication?

No. Psychologists don’t prescribe medication. If I think it could help, I’ll suggest seeing a psychiatrist, and we can keep working together alongside that.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on what you’d like to work on. We’ll sketch a rough plan in the first session and review it together as we go. You can stop whenever you choose.

I’m not sure therapy is for me. Can I still book?

Yes. The first session is a chance to see how it feels to talk with me. If it isn’t the right fit, I’ll suggest other options.
